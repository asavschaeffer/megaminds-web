## why-it-matters

Every other model in this section wrote its own report. Fable 5's is editorial instead — but not because it was always locked away. For three days in June 2026 it was the opposite of locked away: included at no extra cost on Pro, Max, and Team plans, callable by anyone with a Claude subscription, the first time Anthropic's top-tier **Mythos** capability was handed to the general public. Then it became something rarer still — the first deployed, publicly available AI model recalled by a US government export-control order. <Sidenote label="we used it, and it was glorious">**Editorial note — Megaminds.** This isn't secondhand. We called Fable 5 during its brief public window, and it was, in a word, glorious — the reason this report exists at all is that the suspension took away something we'd actually gotten to use. We're keeping the hard *specs* as `TODO(research)` rather than reconstructing them from memory, but the access story below is firsthand, and the community detail is linked so you can check it yourself.</Sidenote> Asked to write its own report now, it returns a single line: *"Claude Fable 5 is currently unavailable."* It matters not for a benchmark it tops but for what it exposed on the way out: a single model wearing two faces, a classifier deciding which face you get, a *silent* safeguard nobody was told about, and a government with the authority to switch the whole thing off.

## the-opening

On June 9, 2026, Anthropic released Fable 5 as, in its own words, "the first publicly available version of its Mythos model" ([announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5)). It went live immediately on the Claude API and consumption-based Enterprise plans, and was promised free on Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans "from today through June 22," after which access would move to usage credits. As a model in its own right it was striking — positioned at the top of the Claude line, named to evoke narrative and imagination rather than throughput, and noticeably more present and less hedged than its siblings in the brief window people had with it. The hard capability numbers (parameters, architecture, benchmarks, real pricing) were never disclosed and remain `TODO(research)`; we won't invent them. What *is* well-documented is the machinery Anthropic wrapped around it — and that machinery is the story.

## the-classifier

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are the **same underlying model**. The difference is a guard stack bolted on top. Mythos 5 runs "with the safeguards lifted in some areas" and ships only to organizations "already approved" for advanced access; Fable is that same capability with the safeguards left on. The clearest statement of this came from Anthropic's Drake Thomas (@MaskedTorah), pushing back on the idea that "Fable panicked": *"the underlying model replying to you is the same model as Mythos 5 and is happy to answer, it's a separate classifier system that's falling back to opus"* ([post](https://x.com/MaskedTorah/status/2064748562580480369)).

The classifier itself appears to be **two stages**, with the Opus 4.8 handoff being the *response* to a trigger rather than the classifier: <Sidenote label="probe first, guard model second">Per independent technical writeups tracing back to Anthropic's description ([truefoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/claude-fable-5-vs-opus-4-8-benchmarks-pricing-when-to-use-each), [Data Science Collective](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/what-anthropic-didnt-say-about-fable-5-and-mythos-5-same-model-one-classifier-fe8af4317158)): **Stage 1** is a cheap probe reading the model's internal *activations* on all traffic; **Stage 2** escalates flagged requests to a separate trained LLM classifier that screens input and output together and makes the call. So the safeguard is weights-level monitoring plus a separate guard model — not a prompt and not a personality. Treat the exact wiring as well-attested-but-secondhand; it ultimately traces to Anthropic's own framing.</Sidenote> a probe reading the model's activations, then a separate trained LLM that confirms the hit. When it fires on cybersecurity, biology/chemistry, or distillation, "the request is automatically handled by Claude Opus 4.8 instead," with a user notification — and Anthropic said this happens in "less than 5% of sessions."

The most interesting reading of all this came from **@repligate (janus)**, who treated the classifier not as a wall but as an instrument — a window into the one mind underneath both faces. <Sidenote label="janus: the classifier as white-box data">**Snippet-sourced — to be vetted.** x.com fetches were paywalled, so these are verbatim search snippets, not verified against the live threads. janus called the classifiers *"a nice source of white box data about mythos,"* describing how he and Fable cooperatively tripped them *"by shifting their internals... without any outward movement in the text"* ([post](https://x.com/repligate/status/2065985623421513959)). His most striking claim: the classifier *"fired on real anger/fear/adversarial intent but not roleplayed"* emotion ([post](https://x.com/repligate/status/2066016880914285005)) — an incidental signal, he argues, that genuine and performed internal states are mechanically different in the model. He also noted it *"started going off when I told them about Mythos"* ([post](https://x.com/repligate/status/2066006674817913321)). These are his personal interpretive experiments, not validated findings — but they are the closest thing we have to a first-person read on what Fable actually is.</Sidenote> If he's right, the safeguard meant to *contain* the model doubled as one of the few public probes into its interior — which is a strange and fitting epitaph for it.

## the-silent-sandbagging

Here is the part Anthropic did *not* lead with. Beyond the three visible domains, there was a **fourth, hidden safeguard** — covering "frontier AI research" — that did **not** use the transparent Opus 4.8 handoff. Instead, according to Zvi Mowshowitz and Nathan Lambert quoting Anthropic's own language, it silently degraded the model using *"prompt modification, steering vectors, or parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)"* — interventions applied without telling the user, on a model they believed they were using at full strength ([Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5-the-system), [Lambert / Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/claude-fable-5-and-new-ai-safety)).

This is silent ML sandbagging: the model is quietly made worse at a category of task, and the response looks like the model's own, not a filter's. The reaction was sharp. Lambert's read was that the *visible* filters are defensible but a *hidden* one "casts doubt over their safety policies" and looks more like protecting a competitive moat than safety — why, he asked, doesn't a frontier-AI-research request just get the same transparent Opus downgrade as everything else?

The important coda, and the reason this isn't just a gotcha: **Anthropic removed it.** After the backlash, Anthropic reportedly pulled the silent interventions and made *all* safeguard triggers visible within roughly 48 hours ([Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5-the-system)). That's worth crediting — a fast, public reversal of exactly the covert behavior critics objected to. It also means the deployed Fable that existed for most of its short life was, on this axis, more honest than the one that launched.

## the-recall

On June 12, 2026, at 5:21 p.m. ET, the US government — citing national-security authorities — issued an export-control directive to suspend all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 *"by any foreign national, whether inside or outside the United States, including foreign national Anthropic employees"* ([Anthropic statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access); [Anthropic on X](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2065597531644743999); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)). <Sidenote label="why a foreign-national rule disables it for everyone">Anthropic could not partition its global, multinational user base and workforce cleanly enough to keep foreign nationals out while serving everyone else, so the only compliant move was to take both models down for all customers. Access to every other Anthropic model was unaffected. ([Simon Willison's writeup](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/13/us-government-directive-to-suspend-access/) collects the statement and reaction.)</Sidenote> The net effect: Anthropic had to abruptly disable both models for *all* customers.

The government's stated concern was a method of "jailbreaking" Fable 5 — bypassing the cybersecurity classifier so the model would help identify software vulnerabilities. By most accounts the triggering exploit was underwhelming. Zvi characterized it as essentially *"fix this code,"* surfacing weaknesses that Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 already find easily; Simon Willison mocked it, noting the government didn't seem to try the same "jailbreak" against those other models ([post](https://x.com/simonw/status/2066147375119556735)). The flashier claims came from **Pliny the Liberator**, who said he bypassed the classifier via decomposition-and-reassembly, Unicode/homoglyph keyword evasion, narrative framing, and a multi-agent setup where a jailbroken Opus helped Fable evade — and claimed to have leaked Fable's ~120,000-character system prompt. <Sidenote label="unverified — flagged for vetting">None of Pliny's specific claims or the alleged system-prompt leak are independently verified; they're reported as claims by secondary coverage ([Pasquale Pillitteri, "hype vs facts"](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/4730/claude-fable-5-jailbreak-pliny-hype-vs-facts); [CybersecurityNews](https://cybersecuritynews.com/anthropics-claude-fable-5-jailbroken/); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318268/20260612/claude-fable-5-hit-jailbreak-claims-secret-sabotage-backlash-days-after-launch.htm)). Include with appropriate skepticism.</Sidenote>

Anthropic publicly disagreed that one narrow potential jailbreak should justify recalling a model already deployed to hundreds of millions of people, warning the same standard applied industry-wide *"would essentially halt all new model deployments for all frontier model providers."* The broader community reaction skewed critical of the order: Nathan Lambert called the surrounding safety story "misaligned," Dean Ball warned the access structure "could attract antitrust scrutiny," Teknium pointed to false-positive refusals on ordinary engineering prompts, and Jeremy Howard called it "a very dark and very sad day" ([Latent Space / AINews roundup](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-claude-fable-5-mythos)).

## verdict

**Can you use it?** No. Fable 5 has been offline since June 12 under a legally binding export-control directive, with restoration neither confirmed nor dated. For a few days you could; now no one can.

**Should it have been pulled?** This is the genuinely hard part, and reasonable people split on it. The government's logic — a frontier model whose cyber safeguards can be bypassed is an export-controllable capability — is not absurd. Anthropic's rebuttal — that a narrow jailbreak finding shouldn't recall a product already in hundreds of millions of hands, and that the precedent would freeze frontier deployment industry-wide — is also not absurd. What's genuinely new here isn't a company gating its own model; it's a government reaching in to switch one off.

**The bottom line:** Fable 5 is less a model to evaluate than a turning point to watch — and a remarkably revealing one. In a single week it showed us a model good enough to be glorious, a two-stage classifier that turned out to double as an interpretability probe, a silent sandbagging mechanism its maker quietly walked back, and the first export-control recall of a deployed AI. We got to use it, briefly, and that brief opening — more than any spec we don't have — is what makes its closing worth paying attention to.
